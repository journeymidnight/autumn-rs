Conversation context:
  Live cluster (4 extent-nodes + 1 PS at 127.0.0.1) was running
  gallery thumb-generation traffic. Operator observed:
    1. `info` showed `disk-3 online=false` on extent-node 9103
       even though the process was alive. manager.log showed flap:
       online → offline → online → offline within 90 s windows.
    2. `forcegc 27 24` printed success but no GC ran. Diagnosis:
       partition 27's log_stream id is 24 (NOT an extent_id). The
       Force branch in background.rs filters extent_ids ∉ sealed_log_extents
       and silently produces an empty `holes` set; there is no warn log.
    3. Per-extent-node RSS at 369–770 MB; previous peak observed at
       ~3 GB during EC conversion of a 3.2 GB extent.

Root cause for the flap:
  Manager's `disk_status_update_loop` (recovery.rs:445) calls
  `conn_pool.call(EXT_MSG_DF, ...)` — the `// F121: bound df at 5 s`
  comment is wrong; the call has no timeout. Single failure marks
  all of that node's disks offline. Under heavy data-plane load
  (CONVERT_TO_EC, COPY_EXTENT) the shared `ConnPool` connection
  to the extent-node is monopolized; DF queues, the background
  reader/writer task in autumn-rpc may exit on a transient TCP
  hiccup, and the next DF returns `ConnectionClosed`. Next 10 s
  tick succeeds → online again. Repeat.

Real fix is not "fix the timeout + add a flap counter" — by the
time the polling loop gets right, alloc has already bounced 3
times against the failed disk because alloc decisions happen
in milliseconds while DF polling is 10 s. The right fix is to
make the data-plane failure signal flow back to manager in real
time and to give per-call alloc retries direct knowledge of which
nodes just failed.
