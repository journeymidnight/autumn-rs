Why:
  F161 audit verified 7 corruption surfaces in hot-path binary RPC
  frames (Append/ReadBytes/CommitLength). All HIGH severity. With V1
  opt-in, the protection sits dormant unless operator explicitly
  enables — most users won't. Flipping default-on means the
  corruption surfaces are closed in production by default.

How:
  One-line semantic flip in crates/rpc/src/frame.rs::v1_encoder_enabled:
    Pre-F165: unwrap_or(false) + matches!(v, "1"|"true"|"yes"|"on")
              — V1 opt-in.
    Post-F165: unwrap_or(true) + !matches!(v, "0"|"false"|"no"|"off")
              — V1 default-on, opt-out via AUTUMN_RPC_FRAME_V1=0.

  Existing frame::tests::encode_decode_round_trip + empty_payload
  updated to assert V1 sizes (HEADER_LEN + payload + 4-byte CRC).

Trade-off:
  ~10% throughput cost on read hot path (HW CRC32C compute at
  ~1 µs per 4 KB payload at SSE4.2). V0 read ~124k ops/s vs
  V1 read ~110k ops/s on tmpfs. Acceptable for corruption protection;
  most production storage systems ship checksums on by default.
